**Review date:** 8 November 2026  
**Jurisdiction:** Kenya (KE)  
**Candidate instrument:** Constitution of Kenya, 2010  
**CARTA status:** `metadata_only`; source inactive; no text imported  
**Decision:** Do not import until the exact current edition and a CARTA-compatible rights basis are documented.

## 1. Official-source route

The National Council for Law Reporting (NCLR), operating as Kenya Law, is the designated publisher and consolidator of Kenya's laws under authority delegated by the Attorney-General. The Parliament of Kenya hosts a page for the *Constitution of Kenya (2010)* and states that the text was published by NCLR with the Attorney-General's authority under section 34 of the Constitution of Kenya Review Act, 2008.

**Recommended canonical-source workflow:** obtain the latest consolidated source directly from NCLR or a gazetted official publication, record the canonical URL and publication/version metadata, and compare the full text against the Gazette and all later constitutional amendment instruments. The Parliament PDF and archival copy are corroborating source leads, not automatic substitutes for this process.

## 2. Rights review — material blocker

Kenya Law's current [Terms of Use](https://kenyalaw.org/terms-of-use/) and [About page](https://kenyalaw.org/about/) state that:

- NCLR's website, systems and content are protected under the Copyright Act (Cap. 130).
- Raw documents as received from the source, in their original unannotated form, are offered for fair and reasonable public use under **Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial 4.0 International (CC BY-NC 4.0)**.
- Automated data scraping, bulk extraction, commercial redistribution and training machine-learning/AI models without an explicit licensing agreement from NCLR are prohibited.

CARTA intends to provide web and native mobile access, paid subscriptions/ad-free upgrades, indexing and search, provision-level excerpts, AI retrieval and summaries, derived plain-language content, and optional offline copies. The non-commercial licence does not on its face grant the full rights CARTA requires. Do not infer that paying for CARTA while using a free legal text is automatically compatible with the non-commercial limitation; obtain written advice and an explicit NCLR licence covering CARTA's use cases.

The Presidential Library's “Public Domain” catalogue field is a potential independent rights lead, but it requires verification of the precise document, provenance and legal basis. A repository label alone is not CARTA legal approval. If relying on that source instead of an NCLR licence, obtain written confirmation from the archive/rights holder and a documented legal opinion covering the exact text and every intended use.

### Permission scope to request

The NCLR request template in [kenya-nclr-licensing-request.md](../rights-requests/kenya-nclr-licensing-request.md) asks for express permission, as applicable, to:

1. Acquire, copy, store, version and checksum the official text.
2. Display and reproduce the text in CARTA web, Android and iOS apps, for free and paid users.
3. Commercially redistribute full text and provision excerpts within CARTA's subscription product.
4. Parse, structure, index, search and expose article-level navigation and snippets.
5. Create and store embeddings; use the text in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG); provide AI-generated answers and summaries grounded in cited provisions.
6. Create plain-language explanations and translations, where separately approved and clearly labelled as unofficial derivatives.
7. Cache and provide offline copies on user devices, with version expiry and update controls.
8. Retain audit copies, hashes, provenance and version metadata needed to prove which edition supported a citation.

Request written terms for attribution, fee or royalty, use limits, permitted excerpts, language editions, update cadence, sublicensing to cloud processors, AI restrictions, offline copies, takedown obligations, termination, expiry and the rights status of future revisions.

## 3. Currentness and amendment control

The Constitution of Kenya, 2010 remains the baseline instrument identified by Parliament and official government pages. This review has **not** certified a consolidated text current to 8 November 2026.

Constitutional amendment Bills are separate from enacted constitutional amendments. Official Parliament materials surfaced in this review show multiple distinct proposals, including:
- the *Constitution of Kenya (Amendment) Bill, 2025* (National Assembly Bill No. 4 of 2025);
- *Constitution of Kenya (Amendment) Bill, 2025* (Senate Bills No. 13 of 2025) and *Constitution of Kenya (Amendment) (No. 2) Bill, 2025* (Senate Bills No. 16 of 2025), both discussed in a Senate committee report dated May 2026; and
- the *Constitution of Kenya (Amendment) Bill, 2026* (Senate Bills No. 7 of 2026).

The official National Assembly tracker dated 5 June 2026 records Senate Bills No. 7 of 2026 as passed by the Senate on 12 May 2026 and referred to the National Assembly. This review did not locate an official, post-5-June-2026 tracker or Gazette confirmation resolving the later status of these and the other proposals. **Do not describe any proposal as an amendment in force unless the enacted Act, presidential assent/publication (where required), commencement and relevant referendum conditions have been verified.**

Before import, conduct a Gazette and parliamentary-history search through the exact review cut-off. Record every enacted amendment, commencement condition, referendum result where applicable, court order and official consolidation notice. Bills and parliamentary proposals belong in the update feed as Bills, not inside the current constitutional text.
