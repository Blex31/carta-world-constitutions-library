PART I: THE STATE AND SOVEREIGNTY

Article 1: Sovereignty

The Republic of Rwanda is a sovereign, democratic, social and secular State.
The sovereignty of the State belongs to the people.
The people exercise their sovereignty directly through referendum or through
their representatives elected by universal suffrage.

Article 2: Territory

The territory of the Republic of Rwanda is composed of the territory of Rwanda
as it existed on 1st October 1962, its internal waters, the air space above and
the subsoil.

The maritime and lacustrine waters, the continental shelf and other maritime
zones belonging to Rwanda are determined by international law and conventions.
The territory of Rwanda is inviolable.

Article 3: National Language and Other Official Languages

The national language is Kinyarwanda.
The official languages are Kinyarwanda, English and French.
Swahili may be used in specific circumstances determined by law.

Article 4: National Symbols

The national symbols of the Republic of Rwanda are:
  1° the national flag;
  2° the national anthem;
  3° the coat of arms;
  4° the national seal.

These national symbols are defined by an organic law.
Any person who desecrates the national symbols shall be punished by law.

Article 5: Capital City

The capital city of Rwanda is Kigali.

Article 6: Separation of Powers

The State is organized on the principle of separation of powers between the
Executive, the Legislature and the Judiciary.

These organs are complementary and collaborate with one another while
respecting their areas of competence as determined by this Constitution.

Article 7: Supremacy of the Constitution

The Constitution is the supreme law of the State.
All laws, organic, ordinary, decrees, orders and other regulations as well as
acts of the State organs, public institutions and local government must
conform to the Constitution.

Any law, act or regulation that is inconsistent with the Constitution is
null and void.

Article 8: Direct Application of the Constitution

The Constitution is directly applicable to all Rwandan citizens, institutions
and organs of the State.

It binds all legislative, executive and judicial organs at all levels of
the State as well as all individuals and legal persons.

Article 9: Hierarchy of Legal Norms

The hierarchy of legal norms in Rwanda is as follows:
  1° the Constitution;
  2° organic laws;
  3° ordinary laws;
  4° Presidential Orders;
  5° Prime Minister Orders;
  6° Ministerial Orders;
  7° other regulations.

In case of conflict, the norm of higher hierarchy prevails over that of
lower hierarchy.

PART VII: GENERAL AND TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS

Article 176: Entry into Force

This Constitution enters into force upon its publication in the Official
Gazette of the Republic of Rwanda.
